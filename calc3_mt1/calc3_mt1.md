## Quick Introduction

Most of the material on this midterm is *Geometric*.  We will be focusing on vectors, in either 2 or 3-dimensional space.  Vectors are the **natural** way to work with geometric objects in higher-dimensional space.

The two most important operations on vectors are the *dot product* and the *cross product*.  Please note that while the dot product is defined for any two vectors of the same size, the cross product is only defined for two 3-dimensional vectors.

After vectors, the most basic geometric objects that we use are lines (which are special *curves*, which are one-dimensional) and planes (which are special *surfaces*, which are two-dimensional).  Both curves and surfaces can be represented as vector-valued functions. 

Given any curve or surface, it is extremely useful to be able to *parameterize* them in different ways.  **PLEASE NOTE** that parameterizations are NOT the same thing as a *coordinate system*.  Very simply, a coordinate system is an underlying structure that allows you to attach an address to every point in the space.  The coordinates themselves are the particular address for the given point.  A parameterization is a way of describing how the points of a particular geometric object (think curve or surface) are travelled through as a function of some external independent variable (usually thought of as time, if the object is a curve).
