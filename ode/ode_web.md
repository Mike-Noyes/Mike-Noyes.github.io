## Online Interactive Texts

I have used the Judson text multiple times when teaching Ordinary Differential Equations.  I am a big fan of it.  It focus equally on the analytical, numerical, and geometric approaches to the subject.  It has embedded Sage cells.  It covers the Laplace Transform.  There is an almost equal amount of discussion of Linear and Nonlinear Equations.  There are lots of homework exercises, some of which are quite challenging.  Each chapter has an in-depth project.  Just an excellent resource.

* [Thomas Judson: *The Ordinary Differential Equations Project*](https://judsonbooks.org/odeproject/odeproject-html/odeproject.html)

I've also used Jiri Lebl's *Notes on Diffy Q's: Differential Equations for Engineers.*  This is a more andvanced text than Judson, but it is a natural continuation.  It goes into PDE's a bit, has a chapter on Fourier Series, and also has a chapter on Power Series Solutions.  About half of the exercises has solutions.  A very good resource.  While the online text doesn't use Sage, there are many Sage examples on the books website.

* [Jiri Lebl: *Notes on Diffy Q's*](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/diffyqs.html)
* [Homepage of *Notes on Diffy Q's*](https://jirilebl.github.io/diffyqs/)

Geoffery Cox's book, *Exploring Differential Equations: An Interactive, Student-Centered Approach* is another very good online book.  While I don't know it as well as the Judson and Lebl books, from what I've seen it is a very good place to start.  There are interactive activities and exercises throughout, which is a plus when trying to learn the material on your own.  I don't think there is any embedded Sage, but I could be wrong.

* [Geoffrey Cox: *Exploring Differential Equations*](https://runestone.academy/ns/books/published/debookrs/main.html)

David Froemke and Heiko Spoddeck have created a *Supplement to Judson's Ordinary Differential Equation Project*, which as the name implies, is not a stand-alone text but is meant to be used in connection with Judson's text listed above.  In the preface they write "We are adding to the work of Thomas Judson's 'The Ordinary Differential Equations Project' by providing additional practice problems that mostly focus on applications," which pretty much sums it up.

* [David Froemkea and Heiko Spoddeck: *Supplement to Judson's ODE Project](https://spot.pcc.edu/~hspoddec/MTH_256/256-Supplement/supplement256.html)
