# Papers, Websites and Other Resources

These are the resources that I used while working on the EML project.

## Articles About Collatz:

An article about Terry Tao and the Collatz Conjecture in [Quanta Magazine](quantamagazine.org):

* [Terry Tao on Collatz Conjecture](https://www.quantamagazine.org/mathematician-terence-tao-and-the-collatz-conjecture-20191211/)
* [Another article from Quanta about the Conjecture](https://www.quantamagazine.org/why-mathematicians-still-cant-solve-the-collatz-conjecture-20200922/)

A survey article by Jeffrey Lagarias, considered the best by many, though it is old.  The first link is to the original in an html version.  The second link is to a pdf of an updated version of the paper.

* [Lagarias Original Paper in html](https://www.cecm.sfu.ca/organics/papers/lagarias/paper/html/paper.html)
* [Lagarias Updated Paper in pdf](lagarias.pdf)

Here are a couple of Bibliographies for the 3x+1 Problem, compiled by Lagarias.  The first covers papers written from 1963-1999 (74 pages!) and the second covers papers from 2000-2009 (42 pages).
	
* [Bibliography 1963-1999](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0309224.pdf)
* [Bibliography 2000-2009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0608208.pdf)


## Websites and Other Resources

Jeffrey Lagarias’s Homepage at the University of Michigan.  If you click on the “Books and Papers” link towards the bottom it will take you to a new page where you will see a link called “3x+1 Problem and Related Problems.”  Clicking on this will take you to a page with all of the papers that Lagarias has written about “the 3x+1 Problem and Related Problems.”  There’s a lot there.

* [Jeffrey Lagarias Homepage](https://websites.umich.edu/~lagarias/)
* [Jeffrey Lagaria Collatz Papers](https://websites.umich.edu/~lagarias/3x+1.html)

A set of slides from a talk that Terry Tao gave about the Collatz Conjecture in late-2019 or early-2020.

* [Terry Tao Talk](https://terrytao.files.wordpress.com/2020/02/collatz.pdf)

Terry Tao’s Blog, which is mind blowingly large.  There’s tons of good stuff on here, so feel free to explore.  Here’s the link to the blog post announcing his recent results on the Collatz Conjecture.  Particularly interesting is the comments section at the bottom where he takes the time to answer many of the questions posed in the comments.

* [Terry Tao Blog Post](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2019/09/10/almost-all-collatz-orbits-attain-almost-bounded-values/)

A page by Eric Roosendall “On the 3x+1 Problem” that contains tons of information and the most current results and records and links to a bunch of other pages.  Worth checking out.

* [Eric Roosendall Page](http://www.ericr.nl/wondrous/index.html#part8)

Probably my favorite math-related website on the entire internet is the Online Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences (OEIS) run by Neil Sloane.  If you ever come across a sequence of integers in any problem and wonder if anybody knows anything about them or has studied them before, or if they’ve popped-up in different contexts, the OEIS should be your very first stop.

* [OEIS](http://oeis.org/) 

Group dedicated to disproving the conjecture: 

* [High Steppers](https://boinc.thesonntags.com/collatz/high_steppers.php)

## Non-Collatz Material

Bill Thurston was one of the great mathematicians of the second half of the 20th century.  He revolutionized many fields of Geometry/Topology and won the Fields medal for his work.  My single favorite article/essay about Mathematics is written by him.  It is called “On Proof and Progress in Mathematics.”  I think that everyone interested in mathematics, especially anyone considering studying higher mathematics should be familiar with it.  The link is below. 

* [Thurston: On Proof and Progress in Mathematics](https://www.math.toronto.edu/mccann/199/thurston.pdf)
