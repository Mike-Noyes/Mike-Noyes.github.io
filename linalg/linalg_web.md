# Resources for Linear Algebra

Where to begin?

## Interactive Online Texts

The book by David Austin is a great first place to start with Linear Algebra.  It presupposes basically zero advanced mathematics and builds up the ideas intuitively.  It also has lots of embedded Sage cells and instructions on using Sage to do Linear Algebra, which is a great way to learn the computational side of things.  Very good exercises throughout.  I have used this etext to teach Linear Algebra and it has worked very well.

* [David Austin, *Understanding Linear Algebra*](https://understandinglinearalgebra.org/ula.html)

After the Austin book, Fitzpatrick's *Linear Algebra: A Second Course featuring Proofs and Python* is a great next stop.  There are interactive exercise throughout the text which provide a great way to check your understanding of the material.  There are a ton of embedded Sage cells, though they almost all use the SymPy package, which makes certain things easier but it involves slightly different syntax that take a little getting used to.

* [Sean Fitzpatrick: *Linear Algebra: A Second Course featuring Proofs and Python*](https://opentext.uleth.ca/Math3410/linear-algebra.html)

Robert Beezer's book, *A First Course in Linear Algebra*, is more abstract and more advanced.  There is way more material here than the previous two sites, though I find it harder to make my way through.  I have wanted to use this book when teaching Linear Algebra, but always end up choosing something else, mainly because I'm worried that my students won't be able to handle it.  This is more an implicit critique of my students than of Beezer's text, but it is something to keep in mind.  This also makes extensive use of Sage, which is a plus.

* [Robert Beezer: *A First Course in Linear Algebra*](https://runestone.academy/ns/books/published/fcla/fcla.html)

Thomas Shemanske's book, *Linear Algebra Companion: A Supplement to Undergraduate Linear Algebra Enhanced by Computation*, is, as the subtitle suggests, not a text to use to learn the subject.  Things are not fleshed-out as much as in the other texts.  But as a **Companion** or **Supplement** to other texts, it is great.  The examples are gone into in detail, mostly using Sage, and are very instructive.  I have used this as a source of interesting examples and problems.  It's also a good place to learn more Sage.

* [Linear Algebra Companion](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~trs/PreTeXtProjects/UGLinearAlgebraCompanion-CLI/output/web/linear-algebra-companion.html)

## Homepages/Academic Pages/Etc

The undisputed world heavyweight champion of Linear Algebra is Gilbert Strang of MIT.  His books are incredible, his website has **TONS** of material, his MIT OCW Linear Algebra courses have dozens of videos.  To really understand Linear Algebra, the best thing you can do is buy his book *Linear Algebra and Its Applications* and visit his website.

* [Gilbert Strang's Homepage](https://math.mit.edu/~gs/)

MIT has so much incredible material connected to Linear Algebra.  In addition to being the academic home of Gilbert Strang, they have all sorts of stuff on the three sites listed below.

* [MIT's Linear Algebra Page](https://web.mit.edu/18.06/www/index.shtml)
* [MIT's OCW Linear Algebra Page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06-linear-algebra-spring-2010/)
* [MIT's Linear Algebra GitHUb Page](https://github.com/mitmath/1806)

After Strang's book mentioned above, my personal favorite Linear Algebra text is Peter Olver's *Applied Linear Algebra*.  While you can't get access to the full text on his website, there is a wealth of material there, related to Linear Algebra and to other areas of Math.  When I taught Linear Algebra in the Summer of 2025, his book and his website were my main sources of inspiration.  Well worth checking out.  His *Partial Differential Equations* book is also great, though necessarily at a more advanced level than the Linear Algebra text.

* [Peter Olver's Homepage](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~olver/)



## Usefull Tools

A matrix calculator.  I can’t emphasize enough how easy this is to use for all things matrix-related.  Check it out!

* [Matrix Calculator](https://matrixcalc.org/)

There are a ton of computational tools at the Linear Algebra Toolkit website.  I haven't used it as much as the Matrix Calculator site listed above, but it is worth exploring.

* [Linear Algebra Toolkit](https://www.math.odu.edu/~bogacki/lat/)




## Misc

Kenji Hiranabe has created some incredible visualizations of some of the most basic and important ideas in Linear Algebra.  They are collected in his GitHub Repo linked below.  English speakers will want to look at the "Art of Linear Algebra", "Map of Eigenvalues," and "Matrix World" pdfs.  They are beautiful.  If you understand Japanese (which I don't!) then you should also check out the Japanese versions.

* [Kenji Hiranabe: *The Art of Linear Algebra* GitHub Repo](https://github.com/kenjihiranabe/The-Art-of-Linear-Algebra/tree/main)
